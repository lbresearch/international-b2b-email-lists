## Market overview

With 2,100,000 verified contacts, the China Business Email Database is one of LeadsBlue's business email list datasets for China. China is the world's 2nd largest economy with over 45 million registered market entities, but it operates under a data regulatory and communication infrastructure that makes standard B2B cold email largely ineffective for foreign companies. The Great Firewall blocks most foreign email services, and WeChat (not email) is the primary B2B communication tool for Chinese business. That said, for foreign companies or joint ventures operating within China, the B2B data landscape in Tier 1 cities (Shanghai, Beijing, Shenzhen, Guangzhou) is dense and internationally accessible. Shanghai is the commercial capital; Beijing is the political and technology policy centre; Shenzhen is the hardware/electronics manufacturing hub; Hangzhou hosts Alibaba's headquarters and a dense e-commerce ecosystem.

**Key business hubs:** Shanghai (Pudong, Lujiazui), Beijing (Zhongguancun tech hub), Shenzhen (tech/hardware), Guangzhou, Hangzhou (e-commerce), Chengdu.

## Outreach strategy

B2B outreach into Chinese companies from international companies is best approached via WeChat, trade delegations, or Chinese platform (Alibaba, Made-in-China.com) presence rather than cold email. For the foreign MNC community operating in China, standard cold email works well — these executives are internationally oriented and email-responsive. Approaching Chinese companies at international trade shows or through Chinese chambers of commerce is more productive than cold digital outreach for domestic Chinese decision-makers.

## Compliance

China's PIPL (Personal Information Protection Law 2021) is among the world's strictest data protection frameworks. It requires explicit, separate consent for each data processing purpose, imposes severe restrictions on cross-border data transfers, and carries penalties up to ¥50 million or 5% of annual revenue. The CAC (Cyberspace Administration of China) is the primary enforcement authority. Cold email to Chinese contacts from foreign companies carries material compliance risk unless the contact data was collected with specific documented consent.

## How to use this list effectively

- **Verify before sending.** B2B contact data decays roughly 2–3% per month as people change roles. Run the list through a verification pass to remove stale or invalid addresses before your first send.
- **Segment by industry and role.** Response rates improve sharply when the message matches the recipient's vertical and seniority — use the segment fields to tailor your copy rather than sending one generic message.
- **Warm up the sending domain.** Start with low daily volume from a dedicated sending domain and ramp gradually to protect deliverability.
- **Follow a multi-touch cadence.** Most replies come from the 2nd–4th touch; a short sequence over 2–3 weeks outperforms a single send.
- **Respect compliance.** Include a valid postal address and a working opt-out, and honour unsubscribe requests promptly (see compliance notes above).
